The Food and Nutrition Research Institute identifies all three Philippine samples by scientific name. The aligue composition is for aligue alone. Cholesterol is blank in these four records. [DOST–FNRI G041](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/report/4021), [DOST–FNRI G040](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/report/4020), [DOST–FNRI G039](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/report/4019), [Australian FRDC species table](https://www.frdc.com.au/sites/default/files/products/2019-143-DLD.pdf#page=51)

The 1999 Indian study measured cholesterol at 51.5 mg per 100 g in claw meat and 54.8 mg per 100 g in a separate mud crab sample. Cooking status is unspecified. The authors noted that other sterols can contribute to the result from their method. [Mathew et al., Table 1(b)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0308-8146%2899%2900050-3)

The FAO/INFOODS global compilation identifies *S. serrata* in the species sheet and gives a raw flesh entry, 092015. Its sources describe a mix of body and claw meat, fresh and frozen. The compiled values per 100 g are 78.3 kcal, 14.7 g protein, 1.67 g fat, 1.14 g available carbohydrates, 108.67 mg cholesterol, 231.45 mg sodium, 3.25 mg zinc, 54.6 µg selenium, 0.033 mg B1, 0.217 mg B2, 3.33 mg niacin, 2.85 mg α-tocopherol, 180 mg EPA, and 160 mg DHA. Cholesterol reflects three inputs, with a reported range of 65–142 mg. Retinol, B5, B6, folate, B12 and C in this entry are estimated from a generic crustacean entry. The boiled entry, 092053, is calculated from 092015 using cooking factors. [FAO/INFOODS database](https://www.fao.org/food-composition/tables-and-databases/detail/f-food-composition-tables/en), [Original workbook, NV_sum row 308 and NV_stat rows 1364–1372](https://www.fao.org/3/I6655EN/uFiSh1.0.xlsx)

## Orange mud crab (*Scylla olivacea*)

- In Bangladesh, raw female breast meat averaged 19.24 g protein, 1.46 g fat, and 2.89 g carbohydrate per 100 g. Using the authors’ 4/4/9 factors gives 101.66 kcal per 100 g from rounded means. The same study's separate raw claw meat averaged 18.86 g protein, 1.79 g fat, and 2.96 g carbohydrate, giving about 103 kcal. The authors report whole-body and exoskeleton sample data separately. [Ullah et al., methods and Table 1](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_129349_4ef58862c556eff7599fa576bdcc0bab.pdf#page=4)
- In Indonesia, Karim et al. report cholesterol means of 90.33, 92.67, 95.33, and 100.33 mg per 100 g across four silvofishery treatment groups. The group differences were statistically inconclusive. Use the range from Table 1 because the discussion gives a different lower bound. The conclusion identifies the sample as crab meat. Tissue selection, cooking status, and moisture basis are unspecified in the methods. Other Hasanuddin farm trials give ranges of 84.67–100.33 and 83.99–89.67 mg per 100 g in their abstracts. These ranges represent their own experimental groups. [Karim et al., Table 1](https://doi.org/10.23880/izab-16000265), [Khasanah, Hasanuddin repository](https://repository.unhas.ac.id/id/eprint/12987/), [Feeding frequency trial, Hasanuddin repository](https://repository.unhas.ac.id/id/eprint/29357/)
- Eligible edible-meat vitamin values remained unverified. The relevant orange mud crab studies found in the search measured proximate composition, laboratory antioxidant activity, feed premix, or shell extracts. A vitamin study with edible meat and vitamin assays specifically identifies its specimens as *S. paramamosain*. [Yusof et al., proximate composition](https://ir.unimas.my/id/eprint/24409/), [Karim et al., fermented extracts](https://doi.org/10.1080/19476337.2024.2363923), [Feed formulation study](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/6101800), [Shell carotenoid study](https://jom.unri.ac.id/index.php/JOMFAPERIKA/article/view/5888/0), [White mud crab vitamin study](https://journal.ipb.ac.id/jurnalikt/article/view/40215)
- Omgy et al. measured minerals in combined body and claw samples from Kerala. The methods describe weighing and digesting dried meat. Table 4 gives raw and cooked groups in mg/kg without specifying a wet-weight conversion. Displaying those mineral values per 100 g of fresh meat would require clarification of the denominator. [Omgy et al., methods and Table 4](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/FT/article/download/124316/48470/339458)

## Swimming crab cross-checks

For comparison with the purple mud crab entry, the same ICMR–NIN reference gives *Portunus sanguinolentus* Q002 raw meat 283 kJ, about 68 kcal, 15.36 g protein, and 0.60 g fat. Its retinol is 5.02 µg, D3 0.63 µg, vitamin E 2.06 mg α-TE, and K2 1.11 µg. Calcium is 333 mg, copper 0.84 mg, iron 0.98 mg, magnesium 80.04 mg, phosphorus 193 mg, potassium 252 mg, selenium 37.69 µg, sodium 313 mg, and zinc 3.07 mg. Its cholesterol is 23.18 mg, EPA 6.97 mg, and DHA 67.91 mg. [Indian Food Composition Tables, Q002, Tables 1, 3, 5, and 7](https://www.nin.res.in/ebooks/IFCT2017.pdf)

FAO/INFOODS raw blue swimming crab (*Portunus pelagicus*, 092014) compiles results for male and female body and claw meat from multiple regions. It gives 87.5 kcal, 19.88 g protein, 0.89 g fat, 133 mg cholesterol, 120.33 mg calcium, 405 mg potassium, 258.33 mg phosphorus, 40 mg magnesium, 302.5 mg sodium, 2.45 mg zinc, 0.04 mg B1, 0.07 mg B2, and 3.9 mg niacin per 100 g. Four analytical cholesterol inputs ranged from 61 to 284 mg. Vitamin B12, folate, and vitamin E estimates were borrowed from pooled crustacean values. [FAO/INFOODS original workbook, NV_stat rows 1346–1354](https://www.fao.org/3/I6655EN/uFiSh1.0.xlsx)

## Species with poisoning evidence

- **Mangrove land crab (*Cardisoma carnifex*)**. The historical Philippine handbook reports trace detections of a paralytic shellfish toxin. The handbook gives no species-specific human case history for mild severity, organ-versus-flesh risk, or gastrointestinal symptoms. Its historical food-use record has no consumption safety assessment. Keep the warning visible and exclude food-use nutrition. [SEAFDEC safety handbook, p. 24](https://repository.seafdec.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12066/5991/safis25.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1), [SEAFDEC field guide, p. 90](https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/bitstream/handle/10862/152/ediblecrustacea.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **Floral egg crab (*Atergatis floridus*) and devil crab (*Zosimus aeneus*)**. Philippine BFAR guidance advises against eating either species, even cooked. Both warrant a toxic-species warning and no food-use nutrition. [BFAR advisory via the Philippine Information Agency](https://pia.gov.ph/news/beware-of-deadly-crabs-bfar/), [BFAR Region 5 poisonous crab advisory](https://region5.bfar.da.gov.ph/2023/07/31/bfar-5-raises-awareness-regarding-the-hazards-of-poisonous-crab/)
