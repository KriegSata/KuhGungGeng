# Shellfie edible-portion nutrition audit

Reviewed 25 November 2026. This review found a species-specific **zinc measurement in Asian shore crab muscle**. Calories, protein, total fat, cholesterol and vitamins remain unverified for edible portions of both species. Horn-eyed ghost crab also lacks a verified serving-level mineral measurement in the sources reviewed.

## Asian shore crab (*Hemigrapsus sanguineus*)

### Zinc in wet muscle from Chinese coastal watersheds

Luo et al. sampled wild Asian shore crabs in October 2008 around the northern Bohai and Yellow Seas. The authors explicitly identified *H. sanguineus* and combined equal weights of dissected muscle from each replicate at a site. Table 2 reports concentrations on a **wet-weight basis**, although the laboratory dried aliquots before analysis. The paper gives a wet-to-dry factor. To express its published wet-weight concentrations as milligrams per 100 g, divide the reported milligrams per kilogram by ten. The converted figures below retain the reported geometric means. [Luo et al. 2013, sampling and quantification methods and Table 2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/etc.2136)

| Study group | Zinc in Table 2 | Zinc per 100 g wet muscle |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Upstream northern Bohai Sea watersheds | 27 mg/kg | 2.7 mg |
| Downstream northern Bohai Sea watersheds | 31 mg/kg | 3.1 mg |
| Downstream northern Yellow Sea watersheds | 44 mg/kg | 4.4 mg |
| Both regions combined, downstream | 35 mg/kg | 3.5 mg |

These figures describe the samples from that environmental survey. The authors also measured cadmium, found concentrations varied by site and reported that some samples exceeded the cadmium guidelines they consulted. The publication supports a localized zinc research entry with its study context. It supplies no basis for a blanket serving recommendation or a Philippine food composition value. [Luo et al. 2013, Tables 2–3, Discussion and Conclusions](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/etc.2136)

### Food use and remaining gaps

- Korea’s National Marine Biodiversity Institute records food use of *H. sanguineus*, listed as **무늬발게** or **갈기** in its ethnobiology entry for Jeollanam-do. The U.S. FDA lists **Crab, Asian Shore** as an acceptable market name. These records contain no quantitative nutrient analysis. Korea’s 2023 Composition Table of Marine Products lists 22 crustacean entries, including other crabs, and has no listing under those Asian shore crab names. [Korean species and use record](https://www.mbris.kr/pub/marine/tsearch/tsearchDetail.do?spcTxnId=270000008130) · [FDA Seafood List](https://hfpappexternal.fda.gov/scripts/Fdcc/index.cfm?id=Hemigrapsus_sanguineus&set=SeafoodList) · [Korea National Assembly Library, 2023 composition table contents, entries 198–219](https://cloud.nanet.go.kr/search/searchView.do?lodArticleId=BOOK_537970)
- Jungblut et al.’s 2018 study reports seasonal lipid measurements as a percentage of dry **midgut gland** mass. Its fatty-acid values describe the lipid extract. The 2023 paper profiles digestive enzymes and feeding ecology. A U.S. diet experiment also measured lipids from the hepatopancreas as an energy-storage indicator. Those methods leave edible-muscle fat and calorie amounts unresolved. [Jungblut et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps12712) · [Jungblut et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2023.1247263/full) · [BCO-DMO primary experiment dataset and methods](https://www.bco-dmo.org/dataset/648239/Dataset_description.pdf)
- A U.S. estuary study measured total mercury in Asian shore crab for pollution research. Its table reports mercury on a dry-weight basis and provides whole-body wet mass separately. The data do not add iron, calcium or vitamins to the edible portion record. [Taylor et al. 2012, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3319229/)

## Horn-eyed ghost crab (*Ocypode ceratophthalmus*)

- The FAO Western Central Pacific guide describes local use as food under the older *O. ceratophthalma* spelling, an account consistent with the Philippine SEAFDEC guide. Indonesia’s fisheries master list explicitly lists **kepiting hantu bertanduk** under consumption, with species code **ODK**. The European Commission also lists ODK as a commercial designation. These accounts establish food use or commercial classification and give no edible-portion nutrient values. [FAO species account, printed p. 1153](https://decapoda.nhm.org/pdfs/4113/4113.pdf) · [SEAFDEC field guide, printed pp. 84–85](https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/bitstream/handle/10862/152/ediblecrustacea.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1) · [Indonesia fisheries master list](https://file.data.go.id/sdi/ckan/resources/abf6e05e-2cfa-40de-a2dd-d825cec7fd59/2021-05-20_kepmen-37-tahun-2021-struktur-basis-data-ikan-dan-data-induk-ikan-auten.pdf) · [European Commission species listing](https://fish-commercial-names.ec.europa.eu/fish-names/species/ocypode-ceratophthalmus_en)
- Odhano et al. measured protein-band patterns, enzyme variants and DNA from claw muscle. Neither paper reports total protein per edible serving. Jia et al. identified peptides in nervous and neuroendocrine tissue. Herreid and Full’s energy-pool percentages describe a general brachyuran crab on an ash-free dry basis. Values from the latter two sources cannot establish the calories, protein or fat in this species’ edible meat. [Odhano et al. 2022](https://juniperpublishers.com/ofoaj/pdf/OFOAJ.MS.ID.555911.pdf) · [Odhano et al. 2025, methods and Tables I–II](https://researcherslinks.com/uploads/articles/1687813224PJZ_MH20220721190738-R2_Odhano%20et%20al.pdf) · [Jia et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3684161/) · [Herreid and Full 1988, printed p. 346](https://polypedal.berkeley.edu/publications/014_Herreid_EnergeticsandLocomotion_BioloftheLandCrab_1988.pdf#page=14)
- The 2026 Vietnam study sampled juvenile crabs and analyzed microplastic particles in digestive tracts. It reports useful occurrence and exposure information, with no protein, lipid, vitamin or mineral concentrations in edible tissue. The 2026 taxonomic revision also split the historically broad species concept into three species, so future records from older papers require an identity check in addition to confirming tissue and units. [Nguyen et al. 2026, sampling and analysis methods](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-8929/5/2/65) · [Davie et al. 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/jzs/8882236)

## Other gaps in Shellfie

- **Crucifix crab.** A raw body-and-claw sample from India gives 2.3 g fat and 15.5 g crude protein per 100 g. Two other Indian samples give 0.70 g fat with 56.5 mg cholesterol and 0.76 g fat with 54.2 mg cholesterol per 100 g. The older cholesterol method can include other sterols. The exact fat and B1, B3 and C rows for the Pakistan sample require the full table. The public preview verifies 80.0 g moisture in that sample. [Sachindra, Table 1.8](https://ir.cftri.res.in/150/1/1868_Sachindra.pdf#page=106) · [Mathew et al., Table 1(b)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0308-8146%2899%2900050-3) · [Umer et al., publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0889157521000776)
- **Orange mud crab.** Ferdousi et al. report 79.33 kcal, 12.63 g protein and 1.30 g fat per 100 g wet meat from wild females in the Ganges–Brahmaputra Delta, using body and claw muscle. Their mineral results are on a dry basis, and the study does not give vitamins or cholesterol for that sample. Omgy et al. use an unspecified 100 g denominator in Table 3 and describe EPA and DHA per 100 g of fat in the results. The previously displayed per-100-g-meat EPA and DHA numbers were withdrawn pending clarification. A newer Chinese study explicitly uses dry muscle weight, so its values also need their stated basis. [Ferdousi et al., methods and Table 2](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Nutritional-prospects-and-heavy-metal-risks/991005703768907891) · [Omgy et al., Table 3 and results](https://doi.org/10.56093/ft.v59i4.124316) · [Four-species Scylla comparison, Table 3](https://html.rhhz.net/ZGHYDXXBYWB/html/cec92a82-69f4-4d69-91e1-6f46beb6af68.htm)
- **Giant mud crab.** Both the Philippine G041 and Thai boiled-meat records leave cholesterol blank. Thai FCD record 2030 explicitly describes whole raw frozen soft-shell *S. serrata* (ARO brand) and gives 64 mg cholesterol and 7.50 µg B12 per 100 g of that product, among other values. [PhilFCT G041](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/report/4021) · [Thai boiled-meat record](https://inmu.mahidol.ac.th/thaifcd/foodsearch/food_name_result_nutrient?mode=food_name_result_nutrient&food_id=1158&dbcode=STD&nutrient_name=&value_per_servingsize=100) · [Thai whole soft-shell record](https://inmu.mahidol.ac.th/thaifcd/foodsearch/food_name_result_nutrient?mode=food_name_result_nutrient&food_id=2030&dbcode=STD&nutrient_name=&value_per_servingsize=100)
- **Three-spot swimming crab.** Wilson et al. publish B12 and C amounts in Table 5, but the methods describe oven-dried tissues and the table does not label the wet- or dry-weight basis. The sample also has unusually large reported values for B12 and D. The figures need clarification before they can fill the wet-weight fields. [Wilson et al., methods and Table 5](https://keralamarinelife.in/Journals/Vol5-12/17%20Sheeba%20Wilson%20et%20al.pdf#page=5)
- **Blue swimming, purple mud and red frog crabs.** The Thai food table records for *P. pelagicus* confirmed no measurements of the additional requested vitamins. The Philippine G038 record and existing species-matched records for purple mud and red frog crab still leave their listed vitamin gaps unresolved. [Thai Food Composition Database](https://inmu.mahidol.ac.th/thaifcd/) · [PhilFCT G038](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/report/4018) · [Indian Food Composition Tables, Q005](https://nin.res.in/ebooks/IFCT2017_16122024.pdf) · [Taiwan FDA composition database, J2500201](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/8543)

## Scope of the search for Asian shore crab and horn-eyed ghost crab

Searches combined scientific names and earlier spellings with English terms for food composition, calories, proximate analysis, cholesterol, vitamins, muscle, wet weight and trace elements. Regional searches also used Japanese **イソガニ**, Korean **무늬발게** and **갈기**, Chinese **角眼沙蟹** and **沙馬仔**, Indonesian **kepiting hantu bertanduk**, Vietnamese **còng gió**, and Philippine **agokoy**. Food-table leads included Philippine PhilFCT, Korea’s 2023 marine food table and Japan’s MEXT source tables. The China CDC table titled **sea crab** gives numeric values but does not specify either species. Verification required an identified taxon, the measured part, a wet-weight or explicitly convertible serving basis, and preparation or sample context.
