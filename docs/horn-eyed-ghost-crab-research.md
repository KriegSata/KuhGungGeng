# Horn-eyed ghost crab evidence update

## Identity and Philippine records

- The accepted name is *Ocypode ceratophthalmus* (Pallas, 1772). The older spelling *O. ceratophthalma* appears in catalogues and papers, so both spellings are useful for searches. The 2026 revision separated what had been treated as one widespread species into *O. ceratophthalmus*, *O. urvillii*, and *O. celadon*. The revision includes a live photograph of *O. ceratophthalmus* from the Philippines in Figure 10A. Broad geographic claims in older papers may cover more than one of these three species. [World Register of Marine Species](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?id=207578&p=taxdetails), [Davie et al. 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/jzs/8882236)
- Mature adults have horn-like extensions beyond the cornea and a slightly wider-than-long, finely textured shell. The length of the horns varies with age and sex, and the colour varies among individuals. The revision describes larger adults burrowing higher on sandy beaches, near the high-water mark, and typically emerging around dusk or at night. [Davie et al. 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/jzs/8882236), [Taiwan Ocean Conservation Administration illustrated guide, p. 40](https://www.oca.gov.tw/filedownload?file=publication%2F202403151349330.pdf&filedisplay=%E8%98%AD%E5%B6%BC%E6%B5%B7%E6%B4%8B%E7%84%A1%E8%84%8A%E6%A4%8E%E5%8B%95%E7%89%A9100%E7%A8%AE-eBook.pdf&flag=doc)
- The revised species occurs in the central Indo–West Pacific. Philippine field records include a voucher from Manukan, Zamboanga del Norte, and a 2024 survey that recorded the species at all 11 study sites in Sitangkai, Sibutu, Simunul, and Bongao, Tawi-Tawi. [Lagare et al. 2020, specimen record](https://treatment.plazi.org/GgServer/xhtml/8D6787D9FFB5FFD8FF6D3CB9FEC7FF1B), [Buldiman et al. 2026, Table 3](https://repository.nfrdi.da.gov.ph/server/api/core/bitstreams/74e6d361-d70b-468c-b258-eaa839c8beb3/content)

## Food use, toxicity, and nutrition

- SEAFDEC recorded historical coastal consumption in the Philippines and lists *agokoy* and *agoyokoy* for both the horn-eyed and smooth-eyed ghost crabs. Philippine reporting also documents *agokoy* for the toxic green egg crab, *Atergatis floridus*. BFAR names *A. floridus* in its poisoning advisory, and a laboratory study reported toxicity in *A. floridus* specimens collected in southern Negros. A shared name is a reason for identification caution. [Motoh 1980, pp. 84 and 86](https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/bitstream/handle/10862/152/ediblecrustacea.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1), [Philippine reporting on local names](https://www.philstar.com/business/agriculture/2002/01/20/147730/beware-killer-crabs), [BFAR advisory via the Philippine Information Agency](https://pia.gov.ph/news/beware-of-deadly-crabs-bfar/), [Southern Negros toxin study](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bbb1961/50/3/50_3_593/_article)
- A Vietnamese field study found microplastic particles in dissected stomachs of horn-eyed ghost crabs sampled at two beaches. The measurements describe environmental exposure in those samples. The broader evidence search yielded no verified human poisoning cases or species-specific toxin measurements supporting a mild or occasional toxicity classification. Food safety remains unverified. [Nguyen et al. 2026, Methods and Results](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-8929/5/2/65)
- Searches using both scientific spellings and regional names found no traceable edible-portion sample for calories, protein, fat, cholesterol, vitamins, or minerals. The public Philippine food composition catalogue yielded no matching record. A Pakistani muscle study measured the molecular mass of individual protein bands for identification. The FAO African table groups several crab genera under one heading, leaves the raw meat row blank, and gives figures for a generic meal. [DOST-FNRI Philippine Food Composition Tables](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library), [Odhano et al. 2022](https://www.rosenberglab.net/Odhano2022OFOAJ.html), [FAO food composition table, entries 1282–1283](https://www.fao.org/4/x6877E/X6877E16.htm)

The evidence supports showing historical food use and a shared-name warning without a toxicity rating. The Nutrition tab can state that species-specific measurements are unavailable. A future nutrient entry needs a confirmed species, measured tissue, preparation, serving basis, numeric values, and a traceable source.
