# Ghost crab and mangrove land crab food-safety evidence

Reviewed 25 November 2026.

## Horn-eyed ghost crab

SEAFDEC documents **agokoy** and **agoyokoy** as Cebuano names for both the horn-eyed ghost crab and the related ghost crab *Ocypode cordimana*. The horn-eyed ghost crab appears as *O. ceratophthalma* in the guide. WoRMS accepts *O. ceratophthalmus*, so searches used both spellings. SEAFDEC describes local food use, and a Palawan checklist documents local harvest. These sources describe the species and its use without establishing whether a particular crab is safe to eat. [SEAFDEC field guide, pp. 83–86](https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/bitstream/handle/10862/152/ediblecrustacea.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1), [WoRMS nomenclature record](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?id=1551558&p=taxdetails), [Subang et al., pp. 73–74](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/355655.pdf)

This review found no species-specific toxicology measurement or human case report establishing mild or occasional poisoning from the horn-eyed ghost crab’s flesh or organs. A Vietnamese field study measured microplastics in the crabs’ digestive tracts. It examined environmental exposure, leaving the claimed clinical effect unverified. [Vietnam field study](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-8929/5/2/65)

SeaLifeBase also records **agokoy** as a Visayan name for *Atergatis floridus*, citing Fresco’s 2002 article in the Philippine Bureau of Agricultural Research’s *BAR Chronicle*. Laboratory research identified toxic *A. floridus* specimens in southern Negros. BFAR advises against eating this species, even cooked. The shared name creates a plausible identification risk. The sources establish name overlap, with no documented poisoning case in this review attributed to that overlap. Shellfie can flag the shared name and keep the ghost crab’s toxicity status unverified. [SeaLifeBase vernacular record](https://sealifebase.ca/ComNames/CommonNameSummary.php?autoctr=78835), [Fresco reference record](https://sealifebase.ca/References/FBRefSummary.php?ID=128968&database=FB), [Yasumura et al.](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bbb1961/50/3/50_3_593/_article), [BFAR Region 5 advisory](https://region5.bfar.da.gov.ph/2023/07/31/bfar-5-raises-awareness-regarding-the-hazards-of-poisonous-crab/), [BFAR update via the Philippine Information Agency](https://pia.gov.ph/news/beware-of-deadly-crabs-bfar/)

## Mangrove land crab

SEAFDEC records **kuray** in Hiligaynon and **kagang** or **ung-kog** in Cebuano for *Cardisoma carnifex*. It describes rural food use. The Palawan checklist also records use in some rural areas and notes that the crab is seldom sold in local markets there. [SEAFDEC field guide, p. 90](https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/bitstream/handle/10862/152/ediblecrustacea.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1), [Subang et al., species table and pp. 75–76](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/355655.pdf)

Arafiles’s SEAFDEC handbook names *C. carnifex* among taxa with trace detections of a paralytic shellfish toxin in a 1981 Philippine survey. It provides no human case data establishing mild severity or a specific pattern of stomach symptoms for this crab. The paragraph’s tissue description follows the third, unidentified taxon. The subsequent symptom list describes bivalve poisoning. The documented finding supports **Poisoning risk reported**. Typical severity, tissue-specific risk and frequency in this species remain unverified. [Arafiles, SEAFDEC handbook, p. 24](https://repository.seafdec.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12066/5991/safis25.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

A feeding study observed that the land crab eats detritus and plant matter. It leaves the source of the 1981 Philippine toxin findings unresolved. A Cocos Island, Guam report mentions purging as a local practice in a rodent-control assessment. This review found no Philippine source establishing the practice for these communities or evidence that purging makes the crab safe from the documented toxin risk. [Lee, feeding study](https://micronesica.org/file/721/download?token=i5ctiEDH), [Cocos Island rodent-control report](https://escholarship.org/content/qt96n4n144/qt96n4n144_noSplash_08daef9635cbc44e69032c7f04cfabc0.pdf?t=pj62os)
