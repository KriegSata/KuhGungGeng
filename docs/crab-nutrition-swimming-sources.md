# Swimming, frog and shore crab nutrition sources

Measurements below refer to 100 g of the stated edible portion unless indicated. The serving basis, preparation and source must travel with the value. A blank in a source leaves the value unknown.

## Selenium in Thai market samples

Rueangsri et al. sampled seafood from three markets in Chonburi, Thailand, removed inedible parts and measured total selenium using mass spectrometry. The cooking conditions were water boiling, palm-oil frying and electric-pan grilling. Values describe the concentration in each resulting edible sample. Water loss and other changes in edible mass can affect the concentration. The 2026 speciation study repeats the total selenium measurements while adding information about the chemical forms of selenium. [Rueangsri et al. 2025, methods and Tables 1–2](https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/14/15/2700), [Rueangsri et al. 2026, Tables 1–5](https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/15/12/2052)

| Species | Raw | Boiled | Fried | Grilled |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Blue swimming crab (*Portunus pelagicus*) | 53.1 µg | 42.3 µg | 97.4 µg | 79.8 µg |
| Crucifix crab (*Charybdis feriata*), called musk crab in the paper | 52.7 µg | 56.9 µg | 87.9 µg | 80.9 µg |
| Giant mud crab (*Scylla serrata*) | 71.2 µg | 33.6 µg | 78.0 µg | 73.8 µg |
| Red frog crab (*Ranina ranina*), identified by the Thai name | 8.6 µg | 14.9 µg | 17.3 µg | 7.3 µg |

The Thai selenium paper calls the red frog crab *Ranina vanima* and Pu Juckajun. Thailand's biodiversity office identifies the local name ปูจักจั่น as *Ranina ranina*, and ITIS lists *R. ranina* as the sole species in the genus. The red frog crab identity is an inference from the local and English names and the taxonomic check. [Thai Office of Natural Resources and Environmental Policy and Planning, row 539](https://chm-thai.onep.go.th/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Thailand_Red_Data_2025_Invertebrate.pdf), [ITIS genus record](https://www.itis.gov/servlet/SingleRpt/SingleRpt?search_topic=TSN&search_value=0098408)

## Blue swimming crab

The Philippine G037 record identifies blue swimming crab meat without crab fat and leaves cooking status unspecified. It gives 93 kcal, 19.9 g protein, 0.5 g fat, 2.2 g carbohydrates, 0.10 g saturated fat, 134 mg calcium, 180 mg phosphorus and 342 mg sodium. The vitamin values are 1 µg RAE vitamin A, 0.04 mg B1, 0.07 mg B2, 3.9 mg niacin and 0 mg vitamin C. G038 provides the separately identified Philippine boiled-meat sample, including 118 mg cholesterol. G036 is the separate aligue sample. These three records leave B12 and vitamin D unreported. [DOST-FNRI G037](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/report/4017), [G038](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/report/4018), [G036](https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/fct/library/report/4016)

Older papers from Australia, the Arabian Gulf, Pakistan and the Mediterranean may use *P. pelagicus* for members of a four-species complex. A later revision places *P. armatus* in much of Australia and *P. segnis* from the western Indian Ocean into the Mediterranean. Some Bay of Bengal populations may include *P. reticulatus* or hybrids. Keep those records out of *P. pelagicus* nutrient cards unless the sample identity is resolved. Aquaculture trials that list nutrients added to crab feed and product sheets that identify the ingredient only as *Portunus* spp. do not give species-specific edible-tissue values. [Lai et al. taxonomic revision](https://zenodo.org/records/5342701), [James Cook University aquaculture trial](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/39433/1/JCU_39433-mat-noordin-2011-thesis.pdf)

## Three-spot swimming crab and purple mud crab

The Indian Food Composition Tables give species-specific raw food records from one sampling region each. Table 1 reports energy in kJ. The kcal values below are rounded after dividing by 4.184. Tables 2 and 3 separate water-soluble and fat-soluble vitamins. The E value is in milligrams of alpha-tocopherol equivalents. Table 5 gives minerals and Table 7 cholesterol. [ICMR–NIN Indian Food Composition Tables 2017, Q002 and Q005](https://www.nin.res.in/ebooks/IFCT2017_16122024.pdf)

In a separate Bangladeshi study, the raw male three-spot swimming crab meat sample contained 14.02 g protein, 2.78 g fat, 220.71 mg sodium, 198.70 mg potassium, 46.92 mg calcium, 4.22 mg iron and 62.10 mg phosphorus. The Indian table supplies the vitamin values above. A separate Indian journal paper lists 4.11 mg vitamin D per 100 g for the same species, equivalent to 4,110 µg. That is over 6,500 times Q002's D3 value and appears in a table using a single milligram header across all vitamins. Keep its values out pending author confirmation of units. [Al Mamun et al., Tables 1–2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jafr.2024.100972), [Wilson et al., Table 5](https://keralamarinelife.in/Journals/Vol5-12/17%20Sheeba%20Wilson%20et%20al.pdf)

## Crucifix crab

The accessible Umer et al. study abstract reports 65.5 kcal and 15.1 g protein per 100 g wet crab meat for *Charybdis feriata*. The study includes four species and gives a combined niacin range without assigning values to individual species. Vitamins and exact species-specific mineral values require the full table. The 1999 Indian records, published under the synonym *Charybdis cruciata*, give 54.2 and 56.5 mg cholesterol in two meat samples, with an assay that may also register other sterols. [Umer et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfca.2021.103877), [FAO copy of abstract](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/providers/122535/records/65de14c44c5aef494fd9af21), [Mathew et al., Table 1(b)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0308-8146(99)00050-3), [WoRMS synonym record](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?id=208820&p=taxdetails)

Lu et al. sampled 45 wild female crabs from three Chinese regions and pooled uncooked muscle from the body, claws and walking legs within each group. The table below estimates grams per 100 g fresh tissue by multiplying the reported mean dry-weight percentage by the mean dry fraction. These are conversions of group means. The study reports EPA and DHA as shares of extracted lipid, so those percentages do not serve as directly measured milligrams per portion. A separate Zhoushan study directly measured 5.33 g essential amino acids and 13.43 g total amino acids per 100 g of fresh muscle. [Lu et al., Tables 2–5 and sample methods](https://ccsenet.org/journal/index.php/jfr/article/download/33425/19250), [Zhong, fresh muscle amino acids](https://www.casb.org.cn/EN/Y2015/V31/I2/97)

## Red frog crab

Taiwan FDA record J2500201 describes one combined raw sample of edible parts. It reports 72 kcal, 16.6 g protein, 0.1 g fat, 54 mg cholesterol, 343 mg sodium and 3.64 µg B12. Additional values include 303 mg potassium, 119 mg phosphorus, 74 mg magnesium, 1.6 mg zinc, 19 mg EPA, 19 mg DHA and 2 mg DPA. Vitamin D and vitamin K are blank in the record. The linked publication reports 43.7% as omega-3's share of the crude fat, a separate basis from the dataset's per-portion values. [Taiwan FDA dataset](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/8543), [Official CSV export, record J2500201](https://data.fda.gov.tw/data/opendata/export/20/csv), [Taiwan Ministry of Agriculture species entry](https://kmweb.moa.gov.tw/theme_data.php?id=155&theme=fisheasy), [Taiwan omega-3 database analysis, Table 6](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1661702/full)

An Australian commercial specification gives a 50 g labeled serving and lists 262 kJ, 15.1 g protein and 50 mg cholesterol per 100 g. The manufacturer repeats the figures for both raw and cooked products and provides no analytical methods, so this specification offers limited evidence about preparation differences. [Ocean Pacific Seafoods product specifications SPMTCKD and 14 SPMTGRN](https://thefishgirl.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Ocean-Pacific-Seafoods-Brochure.pdf)

## Asian shore crab

English and East Asian name searches returned species identification, ecology and contaminant studies without a verifiable human-food composition sample. The U.S. FDA entry supplies a market name. The University of Niigata and JAMSTEC identify the Japanese name イソガニ. A field ecology study measured lipid in the digestive gland as an energy-storage indicator. The calorie, vitamin and cholesterol gaps therefore remain. [U.S. FDA Seafood List](https://hfpappexternal.fda.gov/scripts/Fdcc/index.cfm?id=Hemigrapsus_sanguineus&set=SeafoodList), [Niigata University](https://www.sc.niigata-u.ac.jp/sc/sadomarine/marinelife/isogani.html), [JAMSTEC](https://www.godac.jamstec.go.jp/bismal/j/view/9005032), [Marine Ecology Progress Series study](https://www.int-res.com/articles/meps2015/522/m522p181.pdf)

A review cites a 1993 Japanese biological assay that detected low sodium-channel-blocking activity in a digestive-gland sample from the Sanriku coast. It gives no documented human poisoning or estimate of how often it occurs. That limited observation supports further inquiry about the tissue and location. [Review table and reference 146](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-3397/6/2/308)
